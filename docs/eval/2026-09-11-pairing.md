## Notes

Written by hand after the run, which includes **fix rounds 1, 2 and 3** (below). The hand
set was written before any of this code was run and its expectations come from reading
the prose; no pattern was tuned to the hand set (the superscript and exponent rules were tuned against the real PDFs, as rounds 1–3 below record).

The counts above are from a re-run of the three cached PDFs after round 3. The PLOS ONE
row is carried over unchanged: it has no cached PDF, re-measuring it would mean going
back on the network, and nothing round 3 touched can move a row with zero markers in it.

### The hand set

61 passages, **109 expectations**: 96 marker-to-sentence pairs and 13 markers that must
be *reported* rather than paired (an author-year style v0.1 refuses to guess at, a number
no bibliography answers). 107 met. The scorer also closes the set in the other direction:
any marker the extractor reports that a row did not declare counts as an `extra` miss, so
a pattern that starts seeing citations which are not there cannot pass unnoticed. There
are none in this set.

Coverage: plain `[n]`, lists, hyphen/en-dash ranges and a backwards range, a marker
opening a sentence, a marker after the full stop, two markers in one sentence,
paragraph-final markers over 2–4 sentences, `et al.` / `Fig. 2` / `e.g.` / `Dr.` inside
sentences, bracketed non-citations (`[Table 1]`, `[7.4]`, `pH [7.4]`), author-year
markers, `[99]`, `[0]`, `[87]`, a partly-unresolved `[2, 99]`, and numbered, gapped and
`5. References` bibliographies.

**Miss 1 — `num-05`, `[15]`, wrong sentence.** "…after twenty cycles.[15] Later
batches…": a reader pairs `[15]` with the sentence it is glued to, but
`retrieval.sentence_spans` needs whitespace after a terminal stop, so the two sentences
are one to it and the claim is too wide. The marker is still on the right paragraph and
the right reference. Left unfixed on purpose, but not for the reason first written here:
`split_sentences` / `sentence_spans` is used by nothing but `ingest` today, so no other
measurement moves with it. The real reason is the rule itself — a boundary placed before
`[15]` with no whitespace after the stop would also split decimals and version strings
like `v1.2`, cutting a number in half to win one row. The same glued marker at the *end*
of a paragraph (`par-12`) pairs correctly.

**Miss 2 — `num-15`, the author-year half of a mixed citation is never reported.** The
passage cites "(see Smith, 2020; [12])". Spec §9 asks for both: the numeric marker is
resolvable and must be paired, *and* the author-year style must be reported as
unsupported so the reader knows something was skipped. v0.1 reports only the first,
because `find_markers` lets a numeric marker win any overlap and drops the author-year
one entirely. This is **not** a pattern gap — `AUTHOR_YEAR` matches the string on its
own; it is the overlap rule. Fixing it means letting both markers through, which also
changes paragraph scoping (an author-year marker in the carrying sentence blocks a
paragraph-scoped citation), so it is recorded here as an expectation the set declares and
the code misses, rather than changed in passing.

**A bug this set found, and the fix.** `par-03` ends "…revised since then. [29]". The
splitter reads the trailing `[29]` as a fourth sentence, so the paragraph-scoped citation
produced a fourth claim whose text was empty once the marker was stripped — a claim that
could never carry a passage (product rule 1). `claims.pair` now drops a claim with no
word in it, which also covers "([5])" left alone in a sentence.

### Fix round 1 — two product-rule-6 gaps closed

**1. Superscript citations are no longer invisible.** Nature-style citations are printed
as superscripts and flatten onto the preceding word (`…structure prediction51`), so
AlphaFold produced **0** markers and no report that anything had been skipped.
`ingest._line_text` now rewrites a span the PDF itself marks as superscript, holding
nothing but numbers, into the bracketed marker it would carry in another journal.

**2. A year is no longer read as an entry number.** `ingest._ENTRY_START` accepted four
digits for a bare marker, so an ACL bibliography whose entries run `Jacob Devlin, … 2019.
BERT: …` was cut on the *years*: RoBERTa's ~100 references became 3, numbered 2016, 2018
and 2019. A bare marker is now at most three digits. RoBERTa now yields 103 references.

### Fix round 2 — what the review caught

**A bibliography that starts mid-list keeps its printed numbers.** Round 1 had added a
rule that a bare list must open at 1, 2 or 3 or be renumbered by position. That is wrong
for a references section printed from the middle of a list (a split file, a repeated
`References` heading, one page of the list on its own): renumbering it points `[12]` at
the entry printed 11 and checks the claim against the wrong source in silence. The rule
is gone; printed numbers are kept whenever the bare cuts keep counting upward (the
`accepted` rule, +1 to +9), which is what distinguishes numbering from prose, and the
three-digit cap is what keeps years out. Residual: three or more lines that happen to
open with ascending small numbers inside a bibliography would still be read as entry
markers.

**Fewer real citations lost to the exponent guard.** Round 1 treated a superscript on any
token ending in a digit or a short letter tail as an exponent, which silently dropped 14
AlphaFold citations on names like `Uniclust30³⁶`, `PDB70⁶⁶` and PDB codes `6Y4F⁷⁷`. Now
only a *standalone* number takes an exponent (`10³`); digits that end a name do not, and
neither does a space in front of the superscript. That recovers 14 markers in AlphaFold,
13 of them genuine citations; the fourteenth (`clamped L¹` in a formula) is a
mathematical superscript on a single-letter variable and is now read as a citation. A
superscript unit (`km²`, `m³`) would be misread the same way; none occurs in these four
documents, and the trade was made deliberately — losing 13 real citations is worse than
gaining one false marker that a reader can see.

The review round after that found one more the guard was still eating: `OpenMM
v.7.3.1⁶⁹`, where the superscript sits on the last field of a **version string**. A digit
preceded by a full stop is no longer a base, so that citation is recovered (AlphaFold:
125 markers → **126**, 128 claims → **129**; reference 69 is Eastman et al., *OpenMM 7*,
which is what the sentence cites). `10³` is still an exponent. The new cost is the
decimal: `0.5³` is now read as `[3]`, the same visible-false-marker trade made above.

**The footnote guard now sees past a closing quote.** A superscript printed after
punctuation is a footnote marker, not a citation (`…and code.¹`, `…training,⁸` — ACL and
Chicago set them that way, while Vancouver and Nature bind a citation to the word:
`…prediction51.`). Whitespace and closing quotes are stripped before that test, so
`…herself.”¹` is recognised too; a closing bracket is not, because `(PDB)⁵` is how Nature
prints a citation on a parenthesised term. Counted **by hand** against the printed pages
— these are not harness output — the guard drops 10 footnote markers out of 10 and keeps
116 citations out of 116.

### Real PDFs

Four documents. Three were named in the plan; `10.1186/s12859-020-3517-7` was added
because none of the first three exercises bracketed numeric pairing, and a pairing report
with no real numeric document in it would be a report about nothing.

**`arXiv:1907.11692` (RoBERTa) — 88 markers, 0 claims, and that is correct.** Every
marker is author-year (`(Devlin et al., 2019)`, `Yang et al. (2019)`), which v0.1 reports
as `UNSUPPORTED CITATION STYLE` rather than guessing which entry it names. The 10
superscripts in this paper are footnote markers and are correctly left alone; before fix
round 1 they became `[1]`–`[10]` and produced 30 claims citing references the sentences
never named. References: 3 → **103**.

**`10.1371/journal.pone.0308142` (PLOS ONE) — no PDF, therefore no measurement.** None of
the published locations is a PDF: Semantic Scholar and Crossref return the HTML article
page, Europe PMC returns full-text XML (classified `other`). The run fell back to the
extracted text and says so. That text holds **zero** `[n]` markers in 15,825 words — PLOS
renders in-text citations as links whose number is not in the extracted text — and the
HTML flattens the body to 2 paragraphs, while the 93-entry bibliography still splits off
cleanly. A **coverage** failure, reported as its own row and never folded into the rate.

**`10.1038/s41586-021-03819-2` (AlphaFold) — 0 markers before fix round 1, 126 now.** 117
are superscript citations that used to be invisible (`…pipelines51`, `…the network4–6`,
`(PDB)5`, `Uniclust30³⁶`, `v.7.3.1⁶⁹`); they produce 129 claims, 15 paragraph-scoped, and the sampled
pairs read correctly ("match traditional, hand-crafted structure prediction pipelines." →
`[51]`; "Our models were trained on a copy of the PDB downloaded on 28 August 2019." →
`[5]`). Three things still miss:

* **No `References` heading is found in this PDF**, so the reference list stays in the
  body (432 paragraphs) and `references` is 0. With no list, every number resolves and
  nothing can be reported unresolved — the citations are visible but not yet checkable.
* **34 of the claims come from the author byline**: affiliation superscripts on the
  authors' names (`Jumper¹,⁴`) are indistinguishable, line-locally, from a citation on a
  word, so the byline paragraph is read as prose and paragraph-scoped. The remedy is
  block filtering, not a better superscript rule.
* the 9 author-year markers in this file are **false positives** read out of the un-split
  reference list (`D176 (2017)`, `(Springer, 2020)`). They make no claims.

**`10.1186/s12859-020-3517-7` (BMC Bioinformatics) — 150 markers, 164 claims, 19
paragraph-scoped, 0 unresolved against 129 references.** This is the row that measures
numeric pairing on printed two-column text. Twelve evenly sampled pairs from the first
(pre-fix) run of this harness were read against the PDF by hand: eleven sit on exactly
the right sentence and the twelfth is a table row; the sample from this run was re-read
and shows the same picture. What does not work is not the pairing:

* 7 of the 150 markers come from superscripts that are **not** citations — 3 affiliation
  numbers in the author line and 4 footnote markers attached to a word rather than to
  punctuation (`prepositional phrase¹`) — and they account for 6 claims;
* roughly one claim in ten is a **table row** (`[48] "Comparative structures 287 288 0.96
  0.70"`), a column-break fragment (`"successful approach implemented in Java."`) or a
  section heading glued to the first sentence ("Evaluation on the SemRep test collection
  In this study, we used…"). The marker is on the text it follows; the text is not prose.

### What this run does and does not license

0.98 is the rate on hand-written prose with clean sentence boundaries: it says the rules
of spec §9 step 2 are implemented as written, bar the two misses named above. On real
PDFs the remaining failures are all upstream of pairing, and they are now specific:

1. **Non-prose blocks become claims** — author bylines (34 of AlphaFold's 129 claims),
   table rows and page-break fragments (~10% of BMC's). The largest open item for Phase 6.
2. **A missing `References` heading** leaves a paper with no checkable bibliography
   (AlphaFold), so nothing can be called unresolved.
3. **No PDF at any published location** (PLOS ONE), where the HTML fallback carries no
   markers at all.
4. **A citation printed after a full stop** is treated as a footnote and skipped — the
   price of not inventing citations in ACL-style papers.
5. **A mixed citation loses its author-year half** (`num-15`): reported nowhere, because
   the numeric marker wins the overlap.
6. **A cross-bracket range is read as its two ends** (`rng-06`): `[1]-[3]` produces 1 and
   3, and the hand set expects exactly that, while an IEEE reader means 1, 2 and 3. The
   expectation is tool-aligned rather than reader-aligned, and is recorded here so the
   row cannot be mistaken for evidence that the range is understood.

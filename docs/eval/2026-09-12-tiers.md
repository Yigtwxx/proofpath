## Notes

- **`high` is reachable, and the Phase 1 doc was wrong to say it is not.** The
  2026-09-11 note ("with a precision target of 0.85, `high` is never reached") was a
  display artefact: the harness printed cut-points to two decimals, the NLI softmax
  saturates, and a genuine cut of 0.99933 printed as `1.00` — the same string
  `eval.metrics.tier_cutpoints` returns when a target is never met. The harness now
  prints cut-points to six decimals with the number of verdicts that would reach
  them, so a real cut can never again be mistaken for an unreachable one. Nothing
  about the model changed between the two runs; only the reporting did.
- **21 of 135 asserted verdicts (16%) earn `high`, and the 85% is a point estimate,
  not a guarantee.** The cut was *fitted on this split and measured on this split* —
  an in-sample optimum — and it rests on 21 verdicts of which at least 18 were right.
  A 95% Wilson interval on 18/21 runs from roughly **0.65 to 0.95**, so the true
  precision of the `high` tier could plausibly be anywhere in that range. Nothing here
  is held out; a second split (or FEVER, spec §14) would be needed to confirm it.
  Quote this as "calibrated for 85% precision on SciFact dev", never as "85%
  accurate".
- **What a tier claims is "at or above", not "in this band".** The cut-points are
  cumulative from the top — the definition in `eval.metrics.tier_cutpoints` — so the
  guarantee is *medium-or-better verdicts are ≥70% precise*, not *a medium verdict
  is 70% precise*. The band table above shows why the distinction matters: the
  [0.80, 0.90) band alone is only 50% precise on 6 pairs. Reports must never be
  reworded to promise the per-band reading.
- **`medium` lands almost on `decide` (0.457948 vs 0.45), so `low` holds 1 of the 135
  asserted verdicts.** 134 of the 135 decided pairs are `medium` or better, so the
  display is effectively **two tiers on this model** — `high`, and everything else
  that is asserted — with `low` carrying the `NEI` verdicts rather than weak
  assertions. That is what the data says, not a bug: the decided set as a whole is
  71.9% precise, above the 70% target. But it does mean the three tiers currently
  separate "very sure" from "asserted at all" rather than grading three useful levels.
  Revisit when the judge (Phase 9) or a domain-tuned NLI model changes the score
  distribution. The harness prints this as a warning of its own whenever `medium`
  lands within 0.01 of `decide`.
- **The bands below 0.90 are thin.** 4, 5, 4 and 6 pairs: their precisions (1.000,
  0.600, 1.000, 0.500) are noise at that size and no cut-point should be read off
  them. Everything load-bearing sits in the top band, which has 116 pairs.
- **A rule-decided verdict is not the model earning a tier.** The numeric layer
  (spec §10) returns a score of exactly 1.0, so it always lands in `high`. That is
  deliberate — it is an arithmetic contradiction, not an entailment guess — and it is
  why `pipeline.tier_note()` keys off `high >= 1.0` rather than off the presence of
  any 1.0 score, while the harness's "unreachable" line keys off `n ≥ high == 0`: a
  cut of 1.0 that the numeric rule reaches is reachable, but it is not the model
  earning a tier. With this calibration the note is empty and no report carries one.
- **Old cached verdicts are bypassed, not migrated.** `cache.model_id` includes the
  thresholds (decision 7.7), so every row written under `decide=0.5, high=0.9,
  medium=0.7` is simply never read back. No migration is needed.
- **Re-derive this whenever the NLI model or its revision changes** (spec §14). The
  cut-points are properties of one model's score distribution and transfer to no
  other.

### Fix round 1 — the guard that keeps a title-first book out of GHOST

Review found that the new full-name pattern ate the *title* of an entry printed
title-first — `Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning. Springer Verlag, Berlin, 2006.`
has exactly the shape of two people — and, worse, that stripping it told
`looks_unindexed` the entry carried an author list, taking away the state that keeps such
an entry out of `GHOST REFERENCE` (product rule 3). Two changes:

1. `looks_unindexed` now asks `_strip_initials_authors`, which only knows the three
   patterns that need an initial or a year. The full-name pattern can never answer
   "did this entry print a person-style author list?".
2. The full-name strip applies only when what remains still reads like a reference: at
   least **two** title-like segments, the first of them at least **three** words. A real
   entry prints a title *and* a venue; a title-first book leaves only its imprint
   ("Springer Verlag, Berlin, 2006"), which is one comma-separated run with no full stop
   in it. Failing the guard costs nothing — the pair is left where it was, which is what
   the resolver did before task 8.7.

**5 title-first book rows added (271 total, real 145), all real and verbatim.** Result:
`Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning`, `Neural Networks and Deep Learning` and
`Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications` → `RESOLVED (low confidence)` (the title
matches its record; the entry prints no author, so the author field cannot agree);
`Foundations of Statistical Natural Language Processing` and `Speech and Language
Processing` → `AMBIGUOUS`. **None is a ghost**, which is the whole point. The six
two-author rows and the three arXiv rows were re-resolved live under the new guard and
kept their states — 5 `RESOLVED`, and Thorne & Vlachos `RESOLVED (low confidence)` through
the arXiv rescue rather than through stripping (its entry has no venue segment, so the
guard declines and the identifier carries it).

- **Gates after round 1: false-ghost rate 0.0 % on 145 real rows, ghost recall 100.0 % on
  106 fabricated rows, nothing fabricated accepted, no mutated row called a ghost.**
- **The price, recorded rather than hidden.** Because `looks_unindexed` no longer counts a
  full-name list as an author list, a *fabricated* reference written in that form now
  reaches `UNVERIFIED (not in bibliographic indexes)` instead of `GHOST REFERENCE`. That is
  the ghost-recall ceiling of OPEN-ITEMS 7.10 widening by one citation style, and it is the
  right way round: rule 3 outranks recall, and an honest "not in the indexes" is never the
  accusation a ghost call is.
- Reproduce: `uv run python scripts/eval_ghosts.py --out docs/eval` (live; per-row results

### Fix round 2 — what the `looks_unindexed` guard costs, measured

Round 1 predicted that a *fabricated* reference in the two-author full-name form would
slip from `GHOST REFERENCE` to `UNVERIFIED (not in bibliographic indexes)`. **Three
fabricated rows in that form were added (274 rows, fabricated 109) and the prediction was
only half right**, which is why it was measured instead of left as a claim:

| row | state |
|---|---|
| `Marcus Halvorsen and Priya Raghunathan. 2019. Latent drift correction …` | `RESOLVED (low confidence)` |
| `Elena Vasquez and Tobias Lindgren. 2021. Cross-lingual calibration …` | `GHOST REFERENCE` |
| `Daniel Okonkwo and Hiroshi Tanabe. 2020. Sparse attention routing …` | `GHOST REFERENCE` |

**None of the three became `NOT_INDEXED`.** The form the guard is about prints its year
*between* the names and the title (`First Last and First Last. 2019. Title.`), and that is
consumed by `_AUTHORS_THEN_YEAR` — an initials-path pattern — so `looks_unindexed` still
sees an author list and the ghost call is still available. The shape that really loses the
protection is the same entry **with no year there at all**
(`First Last and First Last. Title. Venue.`), pinned by two offline tests in
`tests/test_resolve.py` rather than by three more live rows: `looks_unindexed` is `True`
and `classify([], raw)` is `NOT_INDEXED`.

**A different defect, found by row 1 and not caused by this task.** It was accepted as
`RESOLVED (low confidence)` against Crossref's record for the *proceedings volume*
`Proceedings of the 13th ACM Conference on Recommender Systems` (`10.1145/3298689`) —
title 1.0, author false, year true. The fabricated entry names that volume as its venue,
so the volume's own title is fully covered by the raw string, and `title_score`'s
proceedings-volume guard does not fire because the volume title shares "recommender" and
"systems" with the fabricated *title* segment. This is the case the guard in `title_score`
was written for, reaching it through a token overlap it did not anticipate. Recorded as a
new open item; not fixed here, because the row is doing exactly what a ghost set is for.
